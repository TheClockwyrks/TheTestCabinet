**Floe** is a single-screen arcade **crossing** game. A small tundra critter must
cross a frozen strait — from the near shore to a row of safe bays on the far
shore —
hopping one tile at a time over lanes of sliding **ice hazards** and then across
open water on drifting **ice floes** that carry it sideways as it rides them.

Its defining idea is the **hunter**. A **polar bear** stalks the critter across
the
*whole* strait: it emerges at the near shore, paths toward the critter, dodges the
same sliding hazards the player must, and swims out after the critter across the
water — staying visible as a silhouette and wake even when submerged. Its one limit
is speed: it moves a touch slower than a cleanly-played critter, so decisive,
forward hopping keeps you ahead — but hesitate, backtrack, get boxed in by a hazard,
or fumble a floe, and it closes and catches you. There is no safe place to wait;
the
only safety is a bay on the far shore, or a fresh crossing you have not yet begun.

Floe is inspired by classic single-screen crossing games but is entirely its own:
original name, look, the pursuing hunter, and its own arctic strait — the game here
is a chase across the ice, not just a puzzle of timing the lanes. It asks a build
for tile-based hopping, lanes of sliding hazards and drifting carry-floes, a hunter
with real pathfinding across the hazard board and the water, a row of goal bays
to
fill, a timer, an 8-level progression, and multiple states — a genuinely harder
front-end task that should separate stronger builds from weaker ones.
