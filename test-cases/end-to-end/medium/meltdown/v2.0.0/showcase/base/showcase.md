Meltdown is a tower defense fought on the floor of a reactor, and the towers are
the walls. Surge intruders pour in through vents cut in the casing and run for
the exhausts on the far side. There is no lane: the route they walk is whatever
route your guns leave them, so every wall you put down is a gun and every gun you
put down is a wall.

Heat is where the power is. An emitter fires harder the hotter it runs, climbing
to full damage at its own redline — but the scale does not stop at the redline,
and a gun carried all the way to the top of it trips offline to cool and leaves a
hole in your line. A tower only sheds heat through the faces that touch open air,
so the tighter you pack the floor the harder your guns cook. Two support
structures sculpt it: a Forge that holds everything it touches near a setpoint,
and a Sink that drains a core with nowhere else left to lose heat.

The replay is half a minute of a real run on Containment. A short wall goes down
and Wave 1 is sent early into it; with only a handful of guns on the floor, the
takings go into making those guns better rather than into buying more. Then a
Forge drops into the gap left in the middle of the wall and is taken to level
III, and that is what tips the line over: Wave 2 cooks two guns past 100 and they
go dark while the surge walks by, a Sink goes in against them, and the wall grows
out into a cross with the wave still running.

Six emitters test the same idea different ways — the balanced Arc, the twitchy
fast-tripping Stutter, the huge Lance that runs cold until you tuck it away, the
splashing Bloom, the air-only Flak, and the cryo Rime, which runs the rule
backwards and slows hardest while it stays cool. Five modes are on the menu:
Containment at three difficulties, a single hundred-strong onslaught, a
ten-thousand-money flush start, a restricted central build zone, and a one-life
sudden death.
