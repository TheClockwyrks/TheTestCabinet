Shatter is an arcade shooter played on a single wrapping field of deep space with
a star burning at the centre of it. You fly one small ship under pure momentum:
turning and thrusting are all you have, there is no brake, and whatever speed you
build stays with you until you turn around and burn it off. Fly off any edge and
you come straight back in at the opposite one.

The star is what makes the game. It pulls on every rock and every shot, but never
on your ship — so your bullets bend as they cross the middle of the field, and
with a little practice you can curve one right around the star to break a rock
sitting on its far side. The core is solid: a rock that falls into it is thrown
back out from the edge of the field, a shot that reaches it is swallowed, and your
ship grazes around it and slides free.

Rocks come apart rather than vanish. A Large splits into two Mediums, each Medium
into two Smalls, and only a Small leaves the field for good — so clearing a wave
means the field gets busier before it gets emptier, with the fragments coming at
you faster than the rock they came from. Break the last one and the WAVE banner
runs, then a denser, quicker wave arrives behind it.

Eighteen seconds in, the saucer turns up. It weaves across the field, steers
around the star, and takes an aimed shot at you every second and a half. Rocks
pass straight through it and it pays them no attention, so it is simply one more
thing in the air while you are already busy — and two hundred points if you can
put a round through it.

The clip is half a minute of the reference build, played from the title screen:
wave one taken apart down to its last fragment, the banner, the next wave arriving
behind it, and the saucer hunting through it.
