Spectra is a formation shooter played on one band at a time. You fly a lone
resonator-fighter along a lane at the bottom of a starfield while crystalline
drones sweep in from above, assemble into a swaying block, and peel off one by
one to dive at you. Your cannon is tuned to cyan or magenta, and only a matching
shot destroys. The same band is your shield: fire in your own band is absorbed
and charges a resonance meter, fire in the other band kills you, and a full meter
buys a discharge that wipes everything in the air.

Overload changes one rule, and it changes how the whole game feels. A shot of the
wrong band is no longer wasted — it puts a charge on the drone it hit, the
telegraph on that drone fills, and the third charge overloads it. So the shot you
would have held under the other mode is now a decision: take it, and something
happens.

What happens depends on what you hit. An overloaded Shard drops out of the block
and plunges at you, far faster than it would ever dive. An overloaded Flux flips
to its opposite band and sprays a fan of fire on the way. An overloaded Prism
bursts in both bands at once, and if you break it before its shell is gone it
calls in another escort to stand beside it. A Flux mid-shimmer takes nothing at
all, from either band.

Everything you get wrong now bites back, which makes retuning matter more, not
less — and a field of half-charged drones is a field of things about to go off.
Three lives, waves that come in faster every stage, and a forty-drone flyover
every third stage that fires nothing and pays a bonus for a clean sweep.

Everything here was captured from the case's reference build, flown through the
keyboard: half a minute of stage one taken to a clear, with all three reactions
arising in play — a Shard plunging, a Flux flipping and spraying, a Prism
bursting — a Prism opened shell-then-core, and a full meter spent on a discharge
that took six things off the field at once.
