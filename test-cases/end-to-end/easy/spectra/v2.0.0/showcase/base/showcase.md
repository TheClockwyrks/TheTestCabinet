Spectra is a formation shooter played on one band at a time. You fly a lone
resonator-fighter along a lane at the bottom of a starfield while crystalline
drones sweep in from above, assemble into a swaying block, and peel off one by
one to dive at you. Your cannon is tuned to cyan or magenta, and a shot only
destroys a drone of the band you are holding — so half the swarm overhead is
simply not yours to shoot until you retune.

The same band is your shield. Fire coming down at you in your own band is
absorbed harmlessly and charges a resonance meter; fire in the other band kills
you. The formation always holds both bands at once, which is the whole squeeze:
the drone you want dead and the bullet you have to survive want you tuned in
opposite directions, and the flip that saves you locks your cannon for a beat.

Three drones press that one idea three different ways. A Shard holds a single
band and dies to one matched shot. A Flux oscillates between the bands on a
telegraphed rhythm, shimmering between them, and while it shimmers nothing can
touch it. A Prism wears both bands at once — a shell over a core of the opposite
band — so you break it open on one band and finish it on the other, and if you
let it dive all the way to the floor it inverts what the entire field reads as
for five seconds.

Fill the meter and you can spend it. A discharge blooms out from the ship and
wipes every diver and every bullet in the air, band-blind, while leaving the
block above untouched — the one move in the game that does not care what you are
tuned to. Clear the wave and the next stage comes in faster, and every third
stage breaks the pattern with a forty-drone flyover that fires nothing and pays a
bonus for a clean sweep.

Everything here was captured from the case's reference build, flown through the
keyboard: half a minute of stage one taken to a clear, with the block flying in
and assembling, dives peeling off it, a Prism opened shell-then-core, the field
inverting, and a full meter spent on a discharge.
