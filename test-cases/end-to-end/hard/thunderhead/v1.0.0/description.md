**Thunderhead** is a real-time, combined-arms **fleet-command** game for the browser,
fought over a procedurally generated world of mountainous islands drowned in an
endless **cloud sea**. You command a fleet — sky-warships, aircraft, and cloud-diving
submersibles — and fight an enemy fleet for command of the sky. Win by destroying the
enemy **flagship**; lose your own and the battle is over.

Its signature is that you play at **two scales at once**. From a tilted tactical
command view you order your whole fleet across the islands; at any instant you can
**drop into direct control of any unit you own — and any weapon station aboard it** —
lay a battleship's guns (fired by class, every turret bearing and in range firing at
one crosshair, each turret and barrel reloading on its own clock), fly a fighter, man
a bomber's turrets, or dive a submersible into the murk — then pull back out to
command while the rest of your fleet fights on under your orders. Steering is always
yours; the guns you are not manning keep firing themselves.

The world's terrain is a forcing rule, not scenery: mountainous islands and floating
crags break the cloud sea, and the dense **murk** below the cloud line is where
submersibles hide. Ridges and islands block sight and gunfire, high ground sees
farther, and deep valleys give room to dive. Three **asymmetric** powers fight over
it — the industrial **Ironbound** (gunpowder artillery, armor, and damage-control
crews), the elegant **Meridian** (high-velocity guns, energy shields, and blinking
aircraft), and the crystalline **Geode** (resonance beams and a healing web projected
by support ships) — each with its own roster, mechanics, and playstyle, all
player-selectable and AI-commanded.

Thunderhead is the catalog's largest build: a procedurally generated 3D world under
an animated cloud sea; a fleet of provided rigid models rendered and articulated in
real time; two-scale command-and-possession control across surface, air, and murk;
a class-based gunnery model with per-turret and per-barrel simulation; a damage model
of armor, shields, resonance, fire, and flooding; detection and a fog of war; a
reinforcement economy; three asymmetric powers; and an AI opponent. It is inspired by
combined-arms naval-command games but is its own game, with an original name, powers,
world, and rosters.
