# Deepcore

You are the last prospector on Vhera Deep, a dead mining world, and the only way
off it is a rocket you have to build yourself. The dropship is scrap and the
launch pad is derelict. Everything the rocket needs is under your feet, and the
only tools you have for getting at it are a handheld drill and a jetpack with a
tank you pay for by the unit.

Digging down is the cheap half. You stand on the camp ground, hold down, and the
drill bites through the tile beneath your boots while the depth meter climbs and
the shaft closes into a narrow bore behind you. Veins show in the walls as you
pass them: dull rust-brown flecks near the surface, muted gold below that, teal
nodules and bright silver seams further down, and now and then a cut jewel that
pays for the whole trip. You step sideways, cut them out, and carry on down,
because the money is always one band deeper.

Climbing back is the half that costs. Falling is free and thrust is not, and
every unit of ore in the bay is weight the jetpack has to lift. A rich haul
climbs slower, burns more, and past the lift limit will not leave the ground at
all until you drop some of it. So the only real decision of a dig is when to turn
around, and the worst place to make it is far down a shaft with a quarter tank
left and a seam in front of you. Get it wrong and the tank empties underground,
which is the one thing down there that kills you outright.

The deep has other ways. Gas pockets look exactly like ordinary rock until the
drill opens one. Lava sears on contact and takes a lump out of the hull to bore
through. Unbreakable boulders have to be dug around. And at the very bottom sits
the Core, where the sample you chip off starts a ninety-second timer that does
not stop for the surface, for a shop, or for anything but the last component
going onto the rocket. Sell, refuel, repair, buy a bigger tank or a harder drill,
and go again.
