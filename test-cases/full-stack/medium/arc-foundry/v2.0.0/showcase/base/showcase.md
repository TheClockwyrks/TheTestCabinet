Arc Foundry is a tower defense played on a derelict substation yard, and it is
built out of parts you never chose. A scrap-press stamps blank rocks, and a rock
rolls a random component type at a random quality the instant it lands. You get
five stamps a level, placing them costs nothing, and every rock is also a wall,
so the maze the Load crawls through is made entirely of rolls you did not pick.

Each level yields exactly one firing structure. Whichever roll you harvest
becomes permanent, every other rock on the yard hardens into an inert blocker,
and committing that harvest is what sends the wave. There is no send button,
only the decision of which of the five to take. Two matching rolls fold into one
a tier higher, up a ladder that runs from pitted Scrap to mirror-chromed
Tesla-Prime, and the right handful of base parts folds instead into one of
twelve combination towers, each with abilities no salvaged component has.

The Load spills from a blown feeder vent and walks the numbered waypoints in
order, so where you build decides how far it has to walk, and the yard refuses
any placement that would seal a leg of the chain. Sparks sprint, Slugs soak,
Clusters arrive in packs, Filaments ignore your maze entirely and fly the
straight line, and anything that reaches the grounding collector costs Grid
Integrity you never get back.

Charge is scarce and buys exactly two things: refinement, which tilts every
future roll away from Scrap, and levels on a combination tower. Bank it into the
press and the yard is better ten waves from now; spend it on the tower in front
of you and this wave goes better. Survive the last wave and an Overload Dynamo
that cannot be killed walks your maze one final time, and everything you land on
it is the run's only score.
