# Arc Foundry

A surge of conductive scrap has blown out of a feeder vent and is crawling across a
derelict substation yard toward a grounding collector. Every unit that reaches the
collector costs Grid Integrity, and when Grid Integrity runs out the yard is lost. You
defend it with a scrap-press that stamps salvaged electrical components out of the rubble.

The catch is that you never choose a component. A stamped rock rolls a random type at a
random quality the instant it lands, and every rock is also a wall. Each level gives you
five rocks and keeps exactly one of them as a firing component; the rest harden into inert
blockers for the rest of the run. So the yard you end up defending is a maze you built out
of rolls you did not pick, and the only real decision is where to drop the next one.

The Load walks the shortest open route through whatever you have built, checkpoint by
numbered checkpoint, so a wall placed well adds a whole extra lap under your guns. It
cannot be sealed off — a drop that would close the route is refused — which turns every
build phase into the same quiet problem: how much longer can this walk get before the wave
starts.

Power comes from folding, not from spending. Two matching components fold into one a tier
higher, up the five-rung ladder from pitted Scrap to mirror-chromed Tesla-Prime, and the
right multiset of ingredients folds into one of twelve combination towers with names like
Rupture Node and Aurora Lance. Charge is scarce and buys only two things: better odds at
the press, and levels on a tower you have already assembled.

Clear the final wave and the run is already won — but one last invincible Overload Dynamo
walks your maze, and every point of damage it soaks up on the way is your Maze Rating. It
is the only score the game keeps, and it measures the maze rather than the player.
