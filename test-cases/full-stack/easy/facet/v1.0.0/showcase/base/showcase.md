Facet is a match-three puzzle on a lapidary's lit bench. Eight columns by eight
rows of cut stones fill the board, swapping two neighbors into a line of three
or more shatters them, the stones above fall into the gap, and fresh ones drop
in from the top — so one swap can touch off a chain that runs long after the
player's hand has left the board.

What makes it its own game is strain. Every clear stresses the stones left
standing around it, and that stress never lets go: a stone carries its cracks
for the rest of the round and takes them with it when it falls. A stone cracked
through is flawed, and a flawed stone goes whenever anything beside it goes.
Ordinary play primes the board a little at a time, and a swap dropped into a
well-primed corner tears a hole clean through it. Three cuts sit on top of that:
a line of four leaves a brilliant that takes its neighbors, a line crossing
another leaves a star that takes its whole row and column, and a line of five or
more leaves a prism that takes every stone of whatever kind it is traded against.

A round has no clock. A meter fills as stones clear, each level asking more than
the last, and the round ends when no swap is left that would shatter anything.

A build carries the whole bench — the nine rules and the chain that resolves
them, the seeded deal, the pointer and keyboard over one selection model, and
five screens — and, because this is a full-stack case, it also draws every stone,
effect and sound it plays as a file it produced during the run.

The media here is one real round on the reference implementation: twenty-two
seconds played with a real mouse, twenty-three moves and forty-four chain steps,
ending on a settled board at level two. The stills are frames of that same round.
