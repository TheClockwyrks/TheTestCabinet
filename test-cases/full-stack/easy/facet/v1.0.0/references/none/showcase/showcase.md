Facet is a match game of cut stones, played on a lapidary's lit bench. Eight
columns by eight rows of gems fill the board. Take hold of a stone, carry it
onto the one beside it and let go: any line of three or more of a kind
shatters, the stones above fall into the gap, and fresh ones drop in from the
top, so a single move can touch off a chain that runs on long after your hand
has left the board.

What makes Facet its own game is strain. Every clear presses on the stones left
standing around it, and that pressure never lets go: a stone carries its cracks
for the rest of the round and takes them with it when it falls. A stone cracked
through is flawed, it goes whenever anything beside it goes, and it is worth
double when it does. Ordinary play primes the board a little at a time, and a
swap dropped into a well-primed corner tears a hole clean through it.

Three cuts sit on top of that. A line of four leaves a brilliant, which takes
its eight neighbors with it. Two lines crossing leave a star, which takes its
whole row and column. A line of five or more leaves a prism, which carries no
kind of its own and takes every stone of whatever kind it is traded against.

A round has no clock. A meter fills as stones clear, each level asks for more
than the one before it and totals up its longest chain and its best single move
before the next board pours in from above, and the round ends when the board
holds no move left that would shatter anything.
