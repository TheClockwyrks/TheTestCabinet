Introduced. A gem-matching puzzle on a lapidary's lit bench: an `8x8` board of
cut stones played with a mouse, a pen, or a finger, where taking hold of a stone
and letting go of it beside its neighbor lines up three or more of a kind, the
stones above fall into the gap, and fresh ones drop in from the top, so one move
can touch off a chain that resolves long after the player's hand has left the
board. A move is offered while the hand is still on it and played only by the
release, so carrying a stone back where it came from takes the move back. What
the round is really made of is strain: every clear presses on the gems left
standing around it, that pressure is never relieved, and a gem cracked through
clears with anything that clears beside it and is worth double, so the board is
always either a resource being primed or one about to be spent. Three cuts sit on
top of it — a run of four leaving a brilliant that takes its neighbors, two runs
crossing leaving a star that takes a whole row and column, and a run of five or
more leaving a prism that takes every stone of the kind it is traded against —
each of them carrying an effect that never stops running, so a cut stone is
picked out of the field by its motion. Nothing on the board teleports: the swap
travels, the clear ripples outward wave by wave, and the stones fall, with the
chain's own cadence waiting on all three. A level ends on a screen that totals up
its longest chain and its best single move before the next board pours in from
above, and the round climbs a level at a time until no legal move is left. The
build produces the game's art, effects, and sound itself during the run, across
the engineless run, the [Simple 2D](/engines/simple-2d/) engine, and the
[Structured 2D](/engines/structured-2d/) engine, on a single `base` variant.
