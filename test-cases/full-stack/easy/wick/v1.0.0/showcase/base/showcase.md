Wick is a survival game about one lamplighter alone in a moth-choked city at
night. Everything in the dark is drawn to the light, and the light is you. The
night runs ten minutes: reach dawn and you have kept it, run out of health and
the lamp goes out.

You only ever move. The lamplighter's tools fire on their own — a taper that
sweeps the air in front of you, lanterns that ride an orbit, a halo that burns
whatever walks into it, bolts that pick their own targets — each on its own
rhythm, none of them waiting for you. So the whole game is where you stand, and
where you stand is the edge of the crowd: close enough that the tools have
something to hit, far enough that nothing gets a hand on you.

Everything you kill leaves a gem, and gems fill the bar under your health.
Every level offers you one of three — a new tool, a new trinket, or another
level on something you already carry — across six tool slots and six trinket
slots, and the run stops dead while you choose. A tool at its top level, beside
the trinket it pairs with, becomes something stronger the next time a chest
opens; chests come off the big ones that arrive on the clock.

The night thickens as it goes. It opens on a single moth a second; two minutes
in it is bats and rats and beetles together and the first elite is on its way;
by the last minutes it is hounds and shades and the Dark, which you do not kill
and simply have to outlive.

The clip is half a minute of one real night, picked up two minutes in with five
tools already on the lamp: the crowd walked in circles, two level-ups taken
mid-fight, and the gems sweeping in behind every kill.
