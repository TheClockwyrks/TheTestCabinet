**Spectra** is a fixed-position formation shooter. You pilot a lone
resonator-fighter along the bottom of the screen while a swarm of crystalline
drones flies in along sweeping paths, assembles into a hovering formation
overhead, then peels off to dive-bomb you — and you clear each wave by destroying
every drone.

Its defining idea is **polarity**. Your cannon is tuned to one of two spectral
bands — Cyan or Magenta — and you flip between them at will. A shot only destroys
a drone of the *matching* band, and your current band is also your **shield**:
enemy fire of your own band is absorbed harmlessly, while fire of the opposite
band is lethal. Because the swarm always holds both bands at once, the drone you
want to shoot and the bullets you must survive constantly pull your choice in
opposite directions. Absorbing fire and landing clean kills charges a
**discharge** that wipes the divers when you are cornered, and the flip that saves
you costs a held beat of fire.

Three drones test that single idea three ways: the **Shard** holds one fixed band,
the **Flux** flickers between both on a telegraphed rhythm you must catch on the
beat, and the **Prism** is a two-band boss you break shell-then-core — and that
flips the colors of the entire field if you let it reach you. Spectra is inspired
by classic formation-shooter arcade games but is entirely its own: original name,
look, the dual-use polarity system, and its own drones — and it deliberately drops
the genre's captured-ship rescue and double-fighter for pure two-band survival.
