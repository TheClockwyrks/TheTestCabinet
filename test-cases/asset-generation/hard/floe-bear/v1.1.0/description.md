**Floe Bear** is the hunter of the Floe arctic-crossing game — the huge white
predator that runs the player down on the pack ice and swims after them through
the open water. Because the pursuit is the game's signature and the bear has to
read as dangerous from every facing (and stay visible against pale ice and under
water), it
is its own asset-generation case: a model draws it as a **sprite sheet** of 18
separate 32×32 frames, all from a top-down camera, one operation at a time — a
heavy, dark-outlined white bear with a four-direction run cycle, a four-direction
mostly-submerged swim with a trailing wake, and an open-maw lunge. It is a creature
on full transparency, composited onto the ice
and water by the game. The recorded operations are regenerated into each frame,
which a reviewer judges against the brief: the read as a big dangerous animal, the
consistent bulk across facings, the four-way run, the readable four-direction
submerged swim, and the lunge are what they weigh, and the named sequences play back
as live animations
in the review UI.
