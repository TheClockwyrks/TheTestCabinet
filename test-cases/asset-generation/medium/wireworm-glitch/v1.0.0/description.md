**Wireworm Glitch** is the corrupted skittering foe of the Wireworm circuit-board
arcade game — the unstable sprite that roams the player's band eating the board's
capacitor nodes. Because its whole character is instability, it is its own
asset-generation case: a model draws it as a **sprite sheet** of 4 separate 32×32
frames, one operation at a time — an angular error-red mass split by cyan/magenta
chromatic-aberration fringes, torn by scanline breaks, and dotted with white
static, jittered to a different corruption in each frame. It is corruption on full
transparency, composited onto the dark board by the game. The recorded operations
are regenerated into each frame, which a reviewer judges against the brief: the
"hostile error" read, the flickering instability across the loop, and the tight
glitch palette are what they weigh, and the named sequence plays back as a live
animation in the review UI.
