**Grace Period** is a game jam — a test case with no specification. Instead of
handing the model a game to build to a bar, it hands over a single **theme** and
asks for a complete, playable, enjoyable game built around it, with the model
producing all of the game's own art, effects, and sound during the run.

The name is a pun with a few layers. A _grace period_ is a forgiving window before
something takes effect — and in an action game, the moment of invulnerability right
after a dodge or a hit is literally called one. "Grace" is also composure under
pressure. All three are the point: this jam is about staying composed inside a hail
of danger, and the small, earned windows that let you.

Unlike a wide-open jam, this theme fixes a **combat feel**: a _strategic_ take on the
"bullet hell", turned deliberately **slow, readable, and tactical** rather than a
twitchy wall of projectiles. Every entry must commit to three things — a **readable,
telegraphed threat** the player can react to; **defense as a real toolkit** (several
distinct answers to incoming fire, not one "don't get hit" button); and **offense and
defense interleaved**, so the player weathers the barrage and picks their own windows
to strike back. Everything else — the setting, the enemies, the exact defensive kit,
the look — is the model's to invent.

Runs are judged the way a game jam is judged: a person plays the entry and grades it
on general categories — **Playability**, **Fun**, **Theme**, **Presentation**,
**Audio**, **Polish**, and **Creativity** — on a five-level scale (💩 → 💎), and
gives the game one overall grade. There is no pass/fail checklist and no reference
build to match; the only brief is _make something playable and enjoyable_.
