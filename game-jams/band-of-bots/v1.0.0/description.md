**Band of Bots** is a game jam — a test case with no specification. Instead of
handing the model a game to build to a bar, it hands over a single **theme** and
asks for a complete, playable, enjoyable game built around it, with the model
producing all of the game's own art, effects, and sound during the run.

The name is a play on "Band of Brothers": the game is a **single-player battle
royale** fought not with a band of brothers but a **band of bots** — a squad of AI
teammates at the player's side, in a match otherwise full of AI combatants. Unlike a
wide-open jam, this one is a capability probe: it fixes the shape of the game and
three hard problems every entry must solve — a **battle royale at scale** (a large
field of AI combatants in a shrinking arena, driven efficiently enough to stay
smooth); a **squad of AI teammates** that reads as competent and worth having, not a
liability to babysit; and **coordination that feels natural**, a slick, low-friction
way to command the squad rather than a menu to wrestle. That last one — the interface
between a human and their AI squad — is the heart of the jam.

Everything about _how_ the battle royale plays — what combatants can do, what fills
the arena, how a match unfolds — is left entirely to the model. There is no prescribed
way it must work.

Runs are judged the way a game jam is judged: a person plays the entry and grades it
on general categories — **Playability**, **Fun**, **Theme**, **Presentation**,
**Audio**, **Polish**, and **Creativity** — on a five-level scale (💩 → 💎), and
gives the game one overall grade. There is no pass/fail checklist and no reference
build to match; the only brief is _make something playable and enjoyable_.
