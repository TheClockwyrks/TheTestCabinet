**Outside the Box** is a game jam — a test case with no specification. Instead of
handing the model a game to build to a bar, it hands over a single **theme** and
asks for a complete, playable, enjoyable game built around it, with the model
producing all of the game's own art, effects, and sound during the run.

The name is the puzzle-solver's creed: _think outside the box._ This is the most
open-ended jam of the set — it asks for a **puzzle game** and fixes almost nothing
else. It is also one of the hardest, because it stacks three design problems at once:
the model has to **invent the challenge** (what the puzzle even is), **build it** so
it plays cleanly, and **author a progression** of levels that ramps.

Three things are fixed, and every entry must deliver all of them: it is a **puzzle
game**, solved by thinking rather than reflexes or chance; it ships **at least eight
hand-designed levels**; and those levels form a **real difficulty ramp** — gentle at
the start, genuinely hard by the end, each asking a little more than the last. A game
need not show the player a difficulty number, but the order the levels play in must be
a deliberate climb — and predicting how hard each puzzle actually is, so that ramp
holds, is a real part of the test. What kind of puzzle it is, and everything around
it, is the model's to invent.

Runs are judged the way a game jam is judged: a person plays the entry and grades it
on general categories — **Playability**, **Fun**, **Theme**, **Presentation**,
**Audio**, **Polish**, and **Creativity** — on a five-level scale (💩 → 💎), and
gives the game one overall grade. There is no pass/fail checklist and no reference
build to match; the only brief is _make something playable and enjoyable_.
